# Suit Builder

Part of the UI docs: [`docs/ui.md`](../ui.md) has the hard rules, tokens, components and the dependency rules every screen follows.

The screen's six modules (`ui/builder.mts`, `ui/builder-header.mts`, `ui/builder-start.mts`, `ui/builder-result.mts`, `ui/builder-manual.mts`, `ui/runs.mts`) share one session, `ui/builder-session.mts`: the selected character and its panel profile, the result, the running job, the saved runs and compare picks, Automatic or Manual, and the knobs, each set through a setter that fires `builderchange` on `document`. They never import each other: what one does for another (render the panel or the result, open a suit in Manual, load the runs) is a command in the session's `commands`, registered by its owner with `provide()`. Today a session is one character, one result and one job.

**Layout** (issues #272, #321). The top bar is the **sentence bar**, one line down to about 1366 px with the sidebar open: "Best suit for **[character ▾]** using **[build ▾]**" (`.b-sent`; the page's `h1`, "Suit Builder", is for a screen reader only), then, held right as one group that never splits (`.b-top-acts`), the large **Build Settings** (`#b-settings`, with its ⌘, hint, shown pressed while the drawer is open) and **Build** (`#b-run`, ⌘↵); both buttons carry `aria-keyshortcuts`. Under it a quieter row (`.b-subbar`) holds **Automatic | Manual** with the hint "Automatic finds the best suit; Manual lets you pick pieces.", and **Saved runs** with its count at the right. On a narrower window the pickers wrap and the group keeps to the right of its row; at 720 px and under the sentence's words, the hint and the key hints go (the pickers and buttons keep their names), each picker takes a full row, and Build Settings and Build share one as large buttons. The two pickers are `components.mts`'s typeahead `combobox` (below). The result takes the full width under it, in its own scroll box (`.b-results`, a size container); side by side (the box 1000 px wide and up), the side column (How the goals went, the character's runs, the first-visit view's side cards) is sticky, so it stays put while the result scrolls and scrolls on its own when it is taller than the box (issue #333; with a block above the result, such as Check your settings, a side column taller than the box starts that much lower and shows its last rows once the result has scrolled); stacked under the result on a narrower box, it scrolls with it. Every setting lives in the **Build Settings drawer** (`#b-drawer` in `#b-drawer-root`, issue #320): the shared modal drawer (`components.mts`'s `bindDrawer`, as Saved runs is), full height at the window's right over a scrim, 560 px wide (`drawer-wide`, `--drawer-w-wide`; the full width on a phone-width window). The focus stays inside it and the rest of the app is inert while it is open; ✕, Esc and a click on the scrim (a click away) close it, the focus back on the Build Settings button, or on the result's score share that opened it (`tuneWeight`). A popover opened from it (a property picker, a chip's checklist, the buff picker) is raised over the drawer and its scrim (`.pop-over-drawer`), so a click in it never closes the drawer, and it closes with the drawer. The result stays behind the scrim while it is open, and a build started from it leaves it open; while it runs the footer's count reads "Building… Close Build Settings (Esc) to see its progress or cancel it.", the progress card and its Cancel being behind the scrim. Its head reads "Build Settings" over the build picker's state line ("from template “Melee” · 2 unsaved changes"), with ✕; under it a built-in template's description (`#b-tpl-info`); then five tabs, a proper ARIA tablist (←/→, Home and End move between them): **Requirements** (the requirements, then **Never wear a bonus to** with a removable chip per forbidden skill bonus and **+ Skill**, its checklist), **Goals** (the tab id stays `weights`: the ranked goals, and under them the weights they set, below), **Buffs**, **Pool** (the switches, Rarity and the chips) and **Advanced** (Search: exact search, restarts, time budget, other suits; Character: Race, STR limit and Casting school; Resist caps). The footer holds **Save profile**, **Save as template** (as the build picker's Save as template…), "N changes since the last build" / "No changes since the last build" (on its own line above the buttons at 720 px and under) and **Build** (`#b-run-foot`). The count is the number of lines `runSettingsDiff` names between the shown run and the panel's settings snapshot, the same diff behind the runs drawer's "Same settings as the panel", so an older run's absent fields, a list in another order or the time budget without exact search count as nothing, and Load these settings leaves none. While the run on screen is the one a build just showed, the count is taken from the settings that build was asked with, since a build answered from an earlier run carries that run's own search knobs; any other run shown (opened from the drawer, that same run reopened, another character) is counted from its own settings, as its banner is. It is repainted 150 ms after the last edit (any field, every search knob included) and is empty while no saved run is shown. A build started from the drawer (its Build, or ⌘↵ in one of its fields) leaves the focus there; the footer's Build is only marked disabled (`aria-disabled`) while it runs, so it keeps the focus. The Build Settings button and ⌘, (Ctrl+, off the Mac) open the drawer while the top bar shows the button (not in Manual or the compare view, nor behind a dialog or the Saved runs drawer), and ⌘, closes it again; opening moves the focus to the selected tab (an open popover takes the first Esc; ⌘, with one open closes both). ⌘↵ in one of its fields builds, as the footer's Build does. Leaving the builder's route closes it. Each tab is a `tabpanel` holding one group or three (`b-sec-<id>`: `req`, `goals` and `weights`, `buffs`, `pool`, then `adv`, `char` and `caps` under Advanced), and `focusIn` and `focusKnob` open the drawer on the group's tab before they focus a control, so Build's own refusals (an Advanced field, a resist cap out of range, a requirement or weight that is not a number) and a server refusal naming a field open it on the right tab. The last tab shown is the ui-prefs `builderTab` (a page's origin changes with every launch of the desktop app, so a view choice is kept by the server, not `localStorage`). Manual closes the drawer and hides Build Settings, Build and the template controls, as the old panel did. The buff picker (below) opens beside the drawer on its left (`besidePlacement`).

**Arriving** (issue #272). **First visit** (`ui/builder-start.mts`, `#b-start`): while no result is shown for the selected character (or No character), the result column is a main column and a side column that wraps under it when narrow (`.b-arrive`). The main column asks "What should <name>'s suit be for?" ("What should the suit be for?" for No character), says that the starting point picked becomes the character's saved settings, then shows four template cards (one row on a wide window, two by two at 1023 px and under): the panel's template as the view is first shown, badged Suggested, then the three other templates that best fit the character's skills (`builder-model.mts`'s `suggestTemplates`: each scored by the skill points of the trained skills, 30 and up, that its name, description, its buffs' casting skills and its casting school name; the player's own templates, newest first, ahead of the built-ins on a tie, then catalog order), each badged Built-in or Yours with a one-line description (a built-in's own, else its Needs line; "Saved Oct 7" for the player's own, from the `savedAt` that Save as and Update write, or "Your template"). The set and its order are fixed while the view is up for the character, a scans refresh included as long as the templates are the same: picking a card never moves one or changes a badge, only the selected state (`aria-pressed`, the panel's template; the selected card has the accent edge on the plain surface, so every badge keeps its pill). A card is the player's starting choice: it applies its template and saves it as the character's settings, as picking it in the build picker does (`builder.mts`'s `pickTemplate`, toast "Caster saved as Dorran's settings."), so a build from it comes back with the header naming that template and no changes. When there is something to lose (the panel differs from its template, or from the character's saved settings, as the build picker's line says) it first asks "Replace <name>'s saved settings with <template>?" (`confirmDialog`; Cancel changes nothing); an untouched profile stays one click. "All N built-in templates" (a link) opens the build picker. Under them a read-only summary of the panel: "<template> will build for" with Needs (`needsLine`: the requirements, the five resists as one "Resists 65" when alike, a soft one marked "(soft)"), Buffs, Pool (`poolLine`: the weapons allowed, the gear pieces scanned, from `GET /api/items` filtered to the gear slots, and the rarity preference) and Search (`searchLine`), each term a link that opens the Build Settings drawer on its tab; and "What matters most": with goals in force (issue #302), the first four ranks (`goalSummary`), one row per rank naming its goals by their goal names, linked ones joined ("2. Hit chance = Defense chance", Swing speed with its swing time: "Swing speed every 1.5 s"), each with what it counts as an importance bar (`.b-imp-goal`, the name wrapping rather than cut off) and opening the Goals tab, then the spare-points choice on its own line ("Spare points: Luck") and "Goals in Build Settings" (or "+ N more goals in Build Settings", counting the goals the hidden ranks hold), "Spare points: not used" for Nothing; with no goals in force, the top four weights by importance (`topWeights`, the resists as one row when they weigh alike) as importance bars in the weight rows' style (`.b-wbar`), each opening its weight in the drawer (`tuneWeight`), then "+ N more in Build Settings" on the Goals tab. Last the big **Build <name>'s suit** (`#b-start-build`, 42 px tall), which is Build. The side column has **<name> is wearing now** (`#b-wearing`, not for No character) with when it was scanned, the five worn resists (held at the Resisting Spells minimum) and up to four gauges, part 2's style without the tick, for the top weighted other properties against their caps; and **Other characters' recent runs** (`#b-other-runs`, `runs.mts`'s `otherRunsCard`), the latest run of up to three other characters still scanned (`otherCharactersRuns`, from every character's run list, fetched once and again only after the runs change), named as the drawer names them, each switching to its character and opening that run the way the drawer does (refused with the drawer's "A build is running" while one runs); hidden with none. The view is redrawn with every panel edit, keeping the focus on the control that had it. **Coming back**: when the builder first shows a character (a page load or a character switch: `selectCharacter`, then `loadRuns(true)`, `arriveAt`), the result column stays blank until the runs are in, whichever list for that character lands first, a refresh started meanwhile included, decides: its newest run that kept a suit (`latestShownRun`) is opened as the drawer opens one, else the first-visit view is drawn; an opened run never replaces a result or a build that came first, and gives way to one started or shown while it loads. A character shown while a build runs gets the first-visit view, and its coming back once the build ends if nothing is shown by then. Opened this way, the run's search knobs (exact, restarts, time budget, other suits, which a profile does not keep) come back into the panel, so a run built from the saved settings comes back with no changes; a refresh of the list or of the scans never opens a run, so a result on screen stays. A result has **<name>'s runs** beside it (`#b-myruns`, `runsCard`): the latest three runs, the shown one in bold, and All saved runs (the drawer); the score breakdown stays in the headline card. While a saved run is shown, the headline has **New build** (`#b-new-build`, before Start from this result) back to the first-visit view, and so does the Saved runs drawer's head (`#b-runs-new`); both are off while a build runs. A saved run's one banner heads the main column (`#b-run-banner`, `paintRunBanner`): "Saved run · today, 10:40 PM" (`dom.mts`'s `fmtRunDay`: today, yesterday, else the date, with the year for another year's) with the settings diff and Load these settings, or, when the inventory changed since the run (`gearChangedSince`, the same test as the drawer's "Inventory changed": the newest scan is not the one the run was built from, its `inventoryStamp`, else for a run without one a scan newer than the run), "This is your build from <day, time>. Your gear changed since: newer scans came in" with ", and N settings changed" (the footer's count) and the diff under it, **Build again ⌘↵**, which loads the run's settings into the panel and builds them, and Load these settings; a scan landing while it is up repaints it. **What changed** (issue #291): for a run that kept its pool (`GET /api/runs/<id>`'s `gearChanges`, `runs-lib.mts` `gearChanges`) the banner lists the changes in place of "newer scans came in" (`gearChangeLines`): "N new pieces you could use" (pieces its pool, rebuilt from the run's settings at the STR limit it was built with, takes now and did not then), with the containers they are in when that is one or two and none is worn; each piece of the suit that moved, "<name> is now worn by <character>", "is now in <character>'s bank", "is now in <character>'s backpack" or "is now in <container>" (one the run's character now wears has not moved); and each one gone, "<name> is no longer in the scans". At most three lines, then "and N more" (four are shown whole), then "N settings changed too." with the diff. With nothing to list, for a manual run, one saved before it, or one the server could not compare (a failed rebuild is logged), the sentence stays as above. The Plan marks the same pieces in Where (`pieceMark`, in the same words: "Now worn by <character>", "Now in <place>") in the warn tone, and "No longer in the scans" (said too of any planned piece the scans no longer have), which has no Highlight or Grab; while Where is hidden (under 1180 px) the mark shows under the piece's name. The banner and the Plan show the same changes, and only while the banner says the gear changed; a scan landing fetches them again and redraws both.

The panel (`ui/builder.mts`) is drawn from state, never read back from the DOM: the session's `profile` holds the template settings, requirements, weights and candidate pool, and its `knobs` object holds the solver fields as typed strings (STR limit, which sits beside Race because it decides which pieces are candidates at all, and the Advanced search options), so a bad value can sit in its field with its error until fixed. A build, a saved profile and a saved run's settings snapshot (`ui/runs.mts`'s `settingsSnapshot`) all read from there. Groups redraw one at a time (`redraw(id)`), so an edit keeps the drawer's scroll and focus. The numbers and words the screen shows (the changes count, validation messages with the allowed range, resist outcomes, other-changes badges, compare rows and best values, run labels and badges) come from `ui/builder-model.mts`, which has no DOM and is tested directly (`app/builder-model.test.mts`); its `KNOB_RANGES` come from the server's own table (`app/run-settings.mts`'s `RUN_SETTING_LIMITS`).

**No character** (issue #12) is an option of the character picker in both modes, under "Without a character" (`#b-char`, value `NOBODY` = `"_nobody"`, defined in `app/scan-schema.mts` beside `isPseudoCharacter` and re-exported by `app/vault-lib.mts`), offered once there is a scanned character. In Automatic it is a character of its own: its panel is a normal `profiles.json` entry under `_nobody` (the first template until Save profile, then what was saved), its builds are saved and reused as runs under `_nobody` (`#/builder/_nobody`, the drawer's `?character=_nobody`), and every list of characters leaves it out (`roster.mts`'s `characterNames`). It builds on raw item totals from the pieces nobody wears: no Resisting Spells minimum, no stats and so no swing, the shard's caps (race human: the Race control is hidden, STR limit stays at 125 by default), and the Faster Casting cap of the Casting school named ("cap 2: no character" from skills); Allow gear worn by other characters adds the worn pieces, and the Resist caps section can still raise a cap. Its buffs are its entry's `buffs.on`, with No character's numbers, the ui-prefs `manualBuffSkills` Manual edits too. Picked in Automatic it also sets Manual to No character (`manualFor: "none"`); picked in Manual it leaves Automatic's character as it is. Before a build the first-visit view asks what the suit should be for, with no worn suit beside it; a result reads "Best suit · No character", its Plan's Now column is empty, its Fetch list holds every piece, the "after the change" card is **Suit totals** (the items' totals, `sheetParts` with no character), and Start from this result opens it in Manual's No character. Grab puts the pieces in the backpack of whichever character the bridge runs on (the toast names it), and is disabled with "Log a character in to grab" while no bridge is online. The labels go through `builder-model.mts`'s `who` ("No character" for `_nobody`).

**The header's pickers** (issue #321). Both are `combobox()`es: a button showing the value (an icon, a title and a subtitle, its accessible name "Character: Dorran" or "Build: Dorran's build", its value in `data-value`) that opens a popover with a search box (the ARIA `combobox`, `aria-activedescendant` on the highlighted option) over a `listbox` of options in labelled groups, each with a title, a subtitle and a right-hand meta; the typed text is matched in the title and subtitle in any case and marked; ↑ and ↓ move the highlight (the current value is checked and highlighted on opening), ↵ picks, Esc closes with the focus back on the button; a footer can hold actions. The **character picker** (`#b-char`) shows the character's name over their build label (issue #336, set on the Characters page), else their top three skills by value (`builder-model.mts`'s `topSkills`), with an initial on one of eight tints (the `--color-area-N` tokens, the same for a name every time); its search ("Find a character, label or skill…") looks in the name, the label and the skills, so a skill still finds a labelled character; its list has every character, with when each last ran (the runs list's newest, `runs.mts`'s `lastRunDays`) or "no runs", then **No character** under "Without a character". Picking one does what the old select did: No character is Manual's in both modes and Automatic's own when picked there. The **build picker** (`#b-tpl`, hidden in Manual) reads "<name>'s build" over a state line (`buildStateText`), never a bare "modified": "from template “Melee”" when the panel equals the template it came from and is saved; "· N unsaved changes" when it differs from the character's saved profile (what Save profile would keep and a template pick would lose); else "from template “Melee”, with N settings changed" when the saved settings differ from the template (all the old "modified" badge compared: it compared the panel with its template, not the saved profile); "not from a template" and "…, which no longer exists" for none and a deleted one. The full text (`buildStateDetail`: "Changed from Melee: … Not saved to Dorran's profile yet: …") is the button's description (`#b-tpl-state`, screen-reader only), and the drawer's head repeats the line. **What changed** (issue #336) is a chip beside the picker (`#b-chg`, hidden while nothing differs): "16 changes from **melee** template" (the template's name in bold) when the panel differs from its template, a dot and "3 unsaved" when it differs from the saved profile. It opens a popover (a dialog, Esc back onto the chip) headed by the same count (the rows it lists, `vault-lib.mts`'s `changesList`) and whether anything is unsaved, then the unsaved edits first, each listed and counted once as template → saved → now (the template's value only when there is one), then the template's other changes by group (Requirements, Weights per point, Pool, Buffs, Character, Search), each a table row of the setting, the template's value, → and this build's (an added requirement in the ok tone, a soft one tagged "soft"; a change with no value pair, such as "excluding Cursed", is its sentence). Each row's name opens Build Settings on that setting (a requirement's field, a weight through `tuneWeight`, else the group's tab), the focus coming back to the chip. Its foot has **Reset to “melee”…** (`pickTemplate`, which asks first) and **Save as template…**. The rows come from `vault-lib.mts`'s `settingsChanges`, the structured form of `settingsDiff` (which is its lines), so nothing parses the text. Its list has the groups **<name>'s own** (the build as it is, checked, and with unsaved changes "<name>'s saved build", which goes back to the saved profile after "Drop N unsaved changes?"), **Start over from a saved template** (each with its description, else "Used by Dorran and Kestrel", else "Saved Oct 7", `templateLine`) and **Start over from a built-in template** (each with its description, else "Ships with Pack Rat"). Picking a template does what a template card does (`pickTemplate`): it applies the template and saves it as the character's settings, first asking "Replace <name>'s saved settings with <template>?" when the panel differs from its template or from the saved profile; picking the build's own template starts it over the same way when it changed. The footer holds **Save as template…** and **Manage templates…**, which opens the **Manage templates drawer** (issue #336, `#tpl-drawer`, `components.mts`'s `createDrawer`, modal like Saved runs, the focus back on the build picker when it closes). At its top, **Save the current build settings as a template…** (`#tm-save`); then **Your templates · N**, a card each (`.tm-card`, `data-template`), by name: a button head (`aria-expanded`) with the name, its description and "changed Oct 7" (`savedAt`), and a chip per character whose saved settings came from it, this build first and marked "Dorran (this build)" ("no one uses it" with none). Open (the build's own card opens first), it shows a one-line summary ("Needs: Resists 65 · Weights: Swing speed, Hit chance · Buffs: none") and **Update <template> with the current build settings** (its confirmation dialog), **Rename** (characters and the build made from it follow the new name), **Duplicate** ("<name> copy") and **Delete…**, which asks in the card: "Delete “x”? Characters made from it (A and B) keep their settings; they just stop showing “from x”." (or "No one uses it.") with Cancel and Delete x. None applies the template. Then **Built in · 17**, read-only, each with Duplicate only. Rename and Duplicate ask for the name (`promptText`): not empty, not taken, not starting with `builtin:`, at most 128 characters (`build-spec.mts`'s `TEMPLATE_NAME_MAX`, which a profiles save (`PATCH /api/profiles`) holds every new template name to as well, naming the one it refuses; a name an older version stored passes as it is, and the characters made from it keep their link when the file loads, migrates or saves); a duplicate gets its own copy of the spec, and a long name wraps inside its card's buttons. The drawer is redrawn after every change, the focus kept on the same control (or the open card's head, or Save) so Esc still closes it. Picking a template asks first only when the panel has something to lose against its own template or saved profile; a character whose template carries no buffs, picking one that carries some, gets that template's buffs in place of its own without being asked, as Apply did before.

**Templates** (issue #212): `templateRefs` keeps the player's first, then the shard's built-ins under their own names, for a new character's default and the MCP error text. A template with a `description` shows a line at the top of the Build Settings drawer (`#b-tpl-info`): a "Built-in" badge for a built-in, the description, "Loads:" with the buffs it turns on, and for a built-in "Source:" with each source page as a link (`builder-model.mts`'s `sourceTitle`), or "UO Alive defaults" when it lists none. Save as and Update store the character's Automatic buffs with the template (`templateSpecFrom(profile, buffs)`, without their numbers, which are the character's); the build picker's line counts a buff list that differs from a template's as one diff line ("Buffs: +Enemy of One, −Consecrate Weapon", `templateBuffsLine`), and a template saved without buffs ignores them. Apply on a template with buffs sets the character's Automatic buffs and, when Manual is for the same character, Manual's list too (its totals set to count buffs when the list has any), as one undo step named "<template> buffs" (`templateBuffs`); with No character Manual is left alone. The MCP tools plan with a template's buffs when none are named.

**Resist caps** is the last group under Advanced. One row per resist: the cap as a number field, "shard cap" when it is the shard's (race-aware), else an accent outline, a "raised from 70" / "lowered from 70" badge and a reset button. A value outside whole numbers 0-150 keeps its error under the row (`resistCapError`) and is held as typed in `capDrafts` (the profile keeps the last good cap), so Build refuses it even with the drawer closed, opening the drawer on the field; a race change drops any override that equals the new race's own cap (`pruneResistCaps`); a cap typed back to the shard's value removes the override (`withResistCap`), so a profile only stores real overrides (`resistCaps`, saved with Save profile, templates and each run's settings snapshot). The result measures resists against the build's caps (`profileResistCaps`): the resist tiles read "86 / 95" with "Cap raised from 70" under them, the outcome lines ("At cap", "N over cap") use the raised cap, the full now → after sheet gets `sheetNode(..., { resistCaps })` (the Characters screen passes none and keeps the shard's), a saved run's badge reads "Fire 86 · cap 95", and compare judges each run by its own caps and adds a "Resist caps" outcome row when any column overrode one. The Requirements note names the character's Resisting Spells minimum and says a resist requirement at or under it is met by any suit (`resistMinimumText`). A resist requirement set above its cap is kept (a saved profile may carry one) but counts only up to the cap, as `effectiveProfile` clamps it for the solver: its row warns "Counts only up to the Fire cap, 70" (`floorCapWarning`), and every view that says whether a requirement is met (the headline, the tiles, run badges, compare) uses `effectiveFloor`, min(floor, cap), so they all agree with the solver. The full sheet's Properties header reads "build cap" instead of "shard cap" when the build overrode one.

**Buffs** (Automatic, issue #12) is the Build Settings drawer's Buffs tab. It lists the buffs by school (issue #272), in `BUFF_GROUPS` order, each group's heading the character's skill in it when the group is a skill (`isSchool`: "skill 68.2", or "not trained"). A buff is a row with its switch (`#b-bf-<id>`, `data-buff`), its name, its effect tags (`buffs.mts`'s `buffEffectTags`: what its definition moves at the character's numbers, each property in its one word from `TAG_WORDS`, "hit chance up · damage up · swing speed up · defense chance down", all five resists moving together as "resists up", a cap change as "defense chance cap up", then its text-only parts, or "no suit effect"; a tag that lowers something for the suit, "defense chance down", is tinted in the warn tone) and a line. Whether it counts is the plan's own answer (`buffAvailability`: `buffNeeds` at the numbers a build plans with, `buffSkillValues` with the typed edits), so the tab never greys a buff a build counts: one the plan blocks is greyed (`.b-bf-cant`) with what it needs, in the warn tone with an alert icon ("Needs Necromancy 99"), and one that leans on a skill the scan lacks, which the plan counts at its default, says "Spellweaving not in the scan: counted at 120"; else the line is its note. A school's heading reads a typed number ("skill 90 (typed)"), else the scan's skill, "not trained" or "not in the scan". Until **Show all** (`#b-bf-all`, kept for the page session) the list holds what the character can use, entries that need no skill included (potions, food, the debuffs), and any that are on; the lead line says Show all adds the ones that need more skill or a skill not in the scan. A greyed buff can still be turned on, except another race's passive. With No character, or a scan that carries no skills, every buff is listed with no skill shown. Turning one on or off redraws the list only (`#b-bf-list`), with the focus kept on its switch, so the picker's anchor stays the same button: Esc closes the picker onto it and its `aria-expanded` follows. Under the list, the help line and **Search and edit numbers** (`#b-buff-add`), which opens the same picker as Manual's (`createBuffPicker`, its ids prefixed `abf` so they never clash with Manual's `bf`; its groups, tags, fields and notes are Manual's, below) in a popover beside the drawer, on its left (`popover`'s `beside`, `besidePlacement`, `--buff-picker-h` tall). In Manual a chip's × keeps the focus in its row (`keepChipFocus`). The picker's note offers Undo after a form replaced another and after **Clear all**. The buffs that are on are kept in the character's profile, its spec's `buffs.on` (`app/build-spec.mts`; a profiles save (`PATCH /api/profiles`) checks them, one form at most, and the page heals a saved list on reading with `normalizeBuffs`, a later form replacing an earlier); the numbers they scale with are the character's, and an edit is the same `buffs.skills` entry Manual edits (`editBuffInputs`); both modes save them at once through `setCharacterBuffs` (`ui/profiles.mts`), which gives a character with no saved settings the ones the panel shows it, and a by-name lookup never reaches a prototype (`Object.hasOwn`, so a character named "constructor" works). No character's numbers are a view choice, ui-prefs `manualBuffSkills`, shared by Manual's and Automatic's No character. Manual's own list (`manualBuffs`) stays apart: it is one list, not one per character, and a step in Manual's undo history.
