# House map

Part of the UI docs: [`docs/ui.md`](../ui.md) has the hard rules, tokens, components and the dependency rules every screen follows.

The House map (`#/map`, issue #10) draws each house a scan captured, one level at a time, from `GET /api/houses` and each house's model (`GET /api/houses/<id>`), both through `ui/houses-data.mts`, which keeps each model by house id and capture stamp until a new inventory or other tiledata, so coming back to the map fetches only the list, the names and the models that changed. Three panes: levels with their status and the player's own areas with chest counts on the left (280 px, its rows spaced `--space-1` apart with `--space-2` padding; an area zooms the map to it); the map in the middle; the details on the right (and a chest's contents beside them, below); one column below 1100 px. The top bar holds the house picker (the deep-linked house; a link to a house that no longer exists falls back to the last house shown, else the house with the most chests), Game angle | Top-down | Stack plan, Contents | Free space, and a pill per level. Every rule and number comes from `ui/house-map-model.mts` (no DOM, tested by `app/ui-map.test.mts`): the projection (`screenX = (x − y)·W/2`, `screenY = (x + y)·W/2 − z·K`), the painter's order, bounds and fit, the joins of a stack with the inventory and the Organize labels, the color modes, the cut-away, callouts, totals and the plain grid. `ui/house-map.mts` builds the SVG with `createElementNS` once per level, view, mode or data change; pan (drag), zoom (the wheel, or the zoom in and zoom out buttons) and fit (the Fit the level button) change only the `viewBox`. Hovering or focusing a stack shows an HTML callout at a fixed size and fades the stacks in front of it, which also stop taking the pointer; stacks are one tab stop (roving tabindex in code order), arrow keys move to the nearest stack that way (one outside the view is brought to its middle), Enter selects, Esc clears; a redraw (a level, an area, a crumb, a reload) keeps the keyboard focus on the same control or, when it is gone, on the nearest stand-in, and Back to the house or Esc from the panel returns it to the stack. Every chest is `role="img"` with "<code> <name>, <fill>" as its name. Floors take `--color-map-<family>` from their material, chests their Organize label color through `safeColor` (always with a `--color-border-strong` edge) or a `--color-map-*` token; every `--color-map-*` token is set in both modes of both theme families. Without a `tiledata.mul` the house is still drawn in neutral colors under a note naming the reason, with a link to Settings › UO folder (house map). A chest a capture saw but no scan opened is drawn dashed and listed as not opened yet: Highlight finds it by the place the capture saw it; Label… and Show items wait for a scan that opens it. Ground containers outside every drawn house are drawn on a plain grid, grouped by facet and distance. **Areas** (issue #10): the model has no automatic rooms (its wall-aware regions stay inside it, for standing spots); the player draws the areas, kept in the house's `house-map.json` entry (`areas`, saved with the whole entry by `PUT /api/house-map/<id>`). Each level lists its areas in order: a `.map-area` button (a swatch in `--color-<color>`, the name, the chest count) that zooms to the area and marks it `aria-pressed` and `.sel` on the map, ✎ (Rename <name>: a field like the house's, Enter or leaving it saves, Esc cancels, a refused name keeps it open with the reason, a redraw keeps the draft) and ⋯ (Redraw, which shows the new shape at once and, if the save is refused, puts the saved shape back and returns to drawing mode with the new one so Enter tries again (a rename or delete under way is closed; an area deleted meanwhile is not saved again, the drawing is dropped and the toast says so); Change color, a popover of the eight colors named Purple, Orange, Teal, Pink, Blue, Yellow, Green and Red (`AREA_COLOR_NAMES`); and Delete, which asks with `confirmDialog` ("Delete <name>?", where its chests go, **Delete area** in danger): Cancel puts focus back on its ⋯, a delete on the next area's ⋯ on that level, else the one before, else + New area); then the rest of the level ("Everything else" once it has areas, else "Whole floor") with a dashed swatch and its count; then **+ New area**. A chest belongs to the first area in list order whose rectangles hold its tile on its level (`areaOfStack`, counts from `levelAreas`). An area saved for a level the house no longer has is left out of the list, the counts, the 32-area cap and the carry-over offer (`liveAreas`), but kept on disk: a save writes the edited list and then those hidden areas as they were (`withOrphans`), so a storey taken away in place and built again gets its areas back. Saves of the entry (a name, an area's name, color, shape, a new area, a delete) run one after another, each built when its turn comes from the list as the one before left it, so a rename still being saved is never undone by a color change or a delete made meanwhile. **Drawing**: + New area (it reads **Drawing…** meanwhile, and the other levels' + New area wait) or Redraw puts a hint bar over the map ("Drag over tiles to draw the area. Shift-drag adds more. Enter to finish, Esc to cancel. Fit shows all." (Fit being the map's fit button), with a screen-reader-only line naming the keys), a crosshair cursor, and makes the map a tab stop of its own (`role="application"`, focused) while stacks leave the tab order; a press and drag selects a tile rectangle (the pointer's point taken back to a tile at the level's floor by `unproject`/`tileAt`, kept 8 tiles off the house by `clampTile`), Shift adds one more (16 at most); the left button no longer pans and the wheel is off during a drag, but the middle button pans and the zoom buttons work. The rectangles show as their covered floor tiles tinted (`coveredCells`) under the walls and stacks, with their outline and the keyboard's cursor drawn over them, and a `.map-draw-size` pill ("w × h = n tiles", `sizeText`). **The keyboard draws too**: the arrow keys move a tile cursor the way they point on screen (`moveCursor`): top-down one tile on the world axes; at the game angle ← and → a whole tile sideways (x ∓ 1, y ± 1) and ↑ and ↓ half a tile up or down, the screen column switching within its pair so two presses go straight, ↓ undoes ↑ and every tile is reachable, Space starts a rectangle at the cursor and ends it (Shift+Space starts one that joins the others), Enter finishes (ending a rectangle under way), Esc cancels (focus back on + New area, or on ⋯ for a redraw); a polite live region outside the panes (`#map-draw-live`) says each size and each rectangle count, emptied and refilled on the next frame so the same words twice are read twice, and says "Drawing canceled." when another level or a crumb ends drawing too. A new area waiting for its name keeps its row through a switch of level. Enter and Esc also work with focus elsewhere on the screen but in a field. A redraw saves at once; a new area asks its name in its row ("Name this area", its color, the field, "n tiles · m containers", Cancel and Save, Save disabled while empty; Enter saves, Esc cancels). **On the map** each area of the level shown is a tint of its floor tiles (its color at 20% through `color-mix`), a 1.5 px line along its union's outer edges (`outlineOf`, a non-scaling stroke), and a `<title>` with its name (none while drawing); areas sit over the floors and under the walls and stacks (a stack above an area still takes the click), and other levels' areas are not drawn. **Name pills**: one per piece of each area (rectangles that touch, 4-neighbor, are one piece: `piecesOf`), in an HTML layer over the map (`#map-pills`, `pointer-events: none`, placed from the SVG's screen matrix again on every pan, zoom, resize and redraw; `pillsOf` runs once per redraw and the fitted texts once per zoom, so a pan only moves the pills, and they are fitted again when the fonts finish loading and when the theme changes, `themechange` from `ui/theme.mts`), so a pill keeps its screen size at any zoom. Each pill is kept 8 px inside the map pane and clear of the zoom buttons, measured once it is in the page (`placePill`); one whose anchor is off the pane is hidden: 12 px text at weight 500, a 1 px line in the area's color on that color mixed 22% into the surface, `--color-text`, `--radius-full` and `--shadow-chip`, sitting just above the middle of the piece's front corner tile (the largest x + y: `frontCorner`; `pillsOf`). A name too long for 92% of its piece's on-screen width is cut with "…" (`fitLabel`, measured on a canvas in the pill's font), and shows as a 10 px dot in the area's color when fewer than 3 characters would fit; both carry the whole name in `title`. The area under the pointer on the map (a stack standing in it counts, `areaOfStack`), its row in the left pane under the pointer or holding focus (`:focus-within`, its ✎ and ⋯ included), and the area zoomed to are lit: the tint 12% stronger, the line 1.5 px thicker, and the pill showing the whole name, stronger, with a halo, over the others. **Hide area labels** (an icon button under Fit the level, `#map-labels`, one fixed name with `aria-pressed`, pressed while they are hidden) hides every pill; tints, lines and hovering stay. The choice is ui-prefs `areaLabels` ("show" or "hide"), applied at load (`applyMapPrefs`), so it survives a reload. The yard tint reads a cell's `indoor` flag. The crumbs read house › level › area › stack (a stack in no area shows "Everything else" or "Whole floor" as text), and a stack's line in the panel and the drawer starts with its area. The plain grid's groups are read-only areas. The eight colors are `--color-area-1` … `--color-area-8`, set in both modes of both theme families, told apart from each other and from every floor color (`app/theme.test.mts`). **Renaming a house** (issue #164): the ✎ inline after the panel's house heading (Rename house, so it follows the last word of a name that wraps) turns it into a name field; Enter or leaving the field (not the window) saves through `PUT /api/house-map/<id>` (with the house's footprint), Esc cancels, a refused name keeps the field open with the server's reason under it until a save or a cancel, and a redraw rebuilds the field with the draft and the reason; focus goes back to the ✎; the picker (`<name> · <facet>, <w> × <h>, <n> containers`), the house crumb and the page title then use the name. An unnamed house whose footprint overlaps a named house no longer captured, on the same facet (a redesign or a move gives it a new id), gets a one-line offer to use that name, and its areas (only the rectangles still on this house) when it had some ("Use the name and areas of …", or "Use the areas …" for an earlier house with areas and no name; `carryOver` and `carryOverText` in `ui/house-map-model.mts`), while this house has neither a name nor areas of its own. **Where the house is** (issue #164): the Location section (headed "Location - <facet> - <x> <y>", `whereTitle`) starts with one line, the plot's center tile (the front steps left out, as the size) and its facet, "<x>, <y> · <facet>" (`whereOf`), with a copy button that copies that line (`copyText`; when the clipboard refuses, the line is selected and a toast says to press ⌘C). No sextant reading and no corners are shown. Under it the section shows 600 × 450 tiles of the facet's overview around the house (`cropAround`, slid inside the facet), fetched once from `GET /api/facet-map/<facet>.png?x0&y0&x1&y1&w=600` and shown as a `data:` URL (the page's CSP allows `data:` images, not `blob:`), so the same request gives the region the server drew (`x-region`, slid inside the facet when the crop reached past it; `parseRegion`) or the 404's reason, with an SVG overlay in that region's tiles (`preserveAspectRatio="none"` over an image of the crop's aspect): a `--color-map-marker` dot on the house (`role="img"`), a smaller `--color-map-marker-other` one on every other captured house of the facet inside the crop, each an SVG `<a href="#/map/<id>">` named by the house (name, else its coordinates), so a click or Tab and Enter opens its map. Marker radii are screen pixels turned into crop tiles from the frame's width (`markerRadii`, kept by a ResizeObserver): a 24 px target per link, and a two-tone focus ring (dark under light) that shows on pale and dark ground. A crop was chosen over the whole facet plus an inset: one image, legible at the panel's width, and Felucca's 7168-tile width would make the house a speck. When there is no image, a muted line in its place says why (`facetMapNote`; a UO folder set in Settings that is gone reads like tiledata's), under the heading and the coordinates, which stay; with no facet known the section is the heading and the coordinates alone. Each facet's size is `FACET_SIZE` in `ui/house-map-model.mts`. The marker tokens are the same in both modes, since the map under them is the game's own. **The world map lightbox** (`ui/world-map.mts`, its rules in `ui/world-map-model.mts`): the small map is an `<img role="button" id="map-where-open">` (Open the world map; click, Enter or Space; the SVG overlay lets the pointer through but its links) that opens `openDialog` with `cls: "world-map-dialog"` (the window less 24 px a side): Esc, the Close button and a click on the backdrop close it, focus goes back to the small map. The view is a box of world tiles shaped like the viewport (`fit`); it starts with the whole facet fitted in its middle, zooms between that and 2 screen px per tile (`zoomView`, about the pointer for the wheel, a pinch arriving as a wheel with ctrlKey; about the house while it is in view for the +/− buttons and the + / - / 0 keys), pans by a drag or the arrow keys (Shift for bigger steps; these keys work wherever focus is in the lightbox, a zoom button included) and never lets the facet leave the view (`clampView`). The zoom buttons' tooltips name their keys. A marker that scrolls out of view while it has focus hands the focus to the map first. A base image of the whole facet is fetched once at the dialog's size (a region of 8192 × 8192 tiles comes back cut to the facet, whose `x-region` gives the facet's real size); when the view is sharper than it, the visible tiles (`overlayRequest`, whole tiles, at screen resolution times the device pixel ratio, at most 2048 a side) are fetched 150 ms after the view settles, older answers dropped by a request id, and laid over the base at the `x-region` they came back with, while the stretched base shows meanwhile. Every captured house of the facet has an HTML marker of a fixed 24 px (`worldMarkers`): the house shown is a labeled `role="img"`, another an `<a href="#/map/<id>">` that closes the lightbox and opens its map (a drag that starts on it opens nothing), labeled once zoomed to 0.5 px per tile and only where its label does not overlap one already placed.

**Search** (issue #10): the box at the top bar's right end (`#map-q`) finds items with the Inventory's own query (`GET /api/items?q=&fields=hits`: the same query answered with lean rows, `item-query.mts` `hitRow`, up to `HIT_LIMIT` a page so one request is normal; 150 ms after typing as in the Inventory; nothing is filtered in the page; an item's tooltip fetches the whole record by serial), and the query is in the route (`#/map/<house>?q=`), so a reload or a deep link keeps it and switching houses keeps it. Each match counts for the container on the floor it is in, through any bags (`floorContainerOf`, `houseHits`). While the house has matches: the box has a gold ring (`--color-highlight`) and an "N in M" count (items, by amount, in containers); matching containers keep their color with a gold outline and their stack stands on a gold base, every other container is a pale ghost; the map zooms to the level's matching stacks when the query changes (`hitsView`, with room above them); each matching stack gets a pinned callout ("Stack I · 1 of 4 match", then per matching container its code, name, place in the stack and the items found), in one row above the stacks in their left-to-right order so leaders never cross, at most three with a "+N more stacks" chip (`calloutRow`; stacks in one screen column put the higher one's card on the left, so their leaders never cross); the callouts are an `aria-hidden` HTML layer (`#map-pins`) placed with the area pills (`paintPills`), and a click on one selects its stack. The left pane counts matching containers per level and area (a gold "N of M" badge, "–" for none), a level pill with matches shows how many, and the panel (nothing selected) lists the level's matches (code, name, area · stack · place, the items with their tooltips, **Show items**; a click on the name selects the stack), other levels with matches, and how many are outside the house with a link to the same search in the Inventory, instead of the totals and Location. A selected stack shows its own panel, the map still highlighted. With no match in this house nothing is ghosted and a note under the box says where they are instead (another house as a link to its map with the query, followed by the container when only one there holds them, "200 in Keep · A1 Wooden Box"; a backpack or bank as text) or "No items match". The top bar wraps when it is too narrow for everything, so the level pills never sit behind the box; the count reserves its own width in the box, and the callouts let the pointer through while an area is being drawn. The count is said through the screen's polite live region; a matching stack's name adds "matches". Esc in the box clears it.

**Stack plan** (issue #10): the View control's third option, the flat and text-first view built for dense rooms. It is not in the route (neither is Game angle | Top-down): it lasts until the screen loads again, and the drawing keeps its angle for the way back. The middle pane holds `#map-plan` instead of the SVG (no zoom buttons, pan, area labels or callouts): a line "Seen from above, north up. …" with "10 stacks · 28 containers" at its right, then a CSS grid of the level (or, with an area zoomed to, of that area's tiles only; the breadcrumb's level goes back). `house-map-model.mts` `stackPlan` works it out: `planTracks` makes a track of each column (and row) holding a stack and squeezes each run of empty ones into one 26 px track (`--plan-gap`) captioned "7 tiles with nothing on them" (`gapText`), turned on its side for a column; tile tracks are `minmax(168px, 1fr)` (`--plan-card`), so about five cards fit and a wider plan scrolls inside the pane (below 1100 px the plan grows with its cards and the page scrolls), never sideways on the page. What the map would zoom to, a "Show on map" stack or a new search's matches, the plan scrolls into view instead (`scrollPlan`: the stack's card, or the first matching card), leaving the zoom for the map when the view goes back. The "Plain colors" note is not shown there. Each tile with a stack is a card: "Stack C" and "4 containers" (`planHead`), then its containers top first (`planRows`), each its code (the column as wide as the card's longest code), its label as a chip in the label's color, the in-game name, and a fill bar with "41/125" out of the container's own maximum, or "Not opened yet" / "Fill unknown"; a tile in the plan with nothing on it is a dashed cell. An area holding a container is a tint and outline in its color behind its cards (a box per rectangle, clipped to the plan), with its name and container count on a pill at its top-left that zooms to it; the areas with no container are named under the plan ("The Yard: no containers."). A level with no stacks reads "No containers on this floor." The plain grid gets the same plan from its tile positions. Between 1100 and 1799 px the levels pane folds as it does for the drawer (`.map-page.plan`), and the area pills carry the areas; below 1100 px the page stacks as usual. New area and ⋯ › Redraw are disabled there, titled "Switch to Game angle or Top-down to draw"; switching to it cancels a drawing or a new area waiting for its name, and a refused redraw does not go back to drawing. Selection, the panel and the drawer are the map's: a click on a card selects its stack (again unselects it), a click on a container row selects it and opens that container in the drawer, whose closing gives focus back to the card; the selected card has the accent outline, and the focused card a focus outline drawn over it. The cards are a list (`ol role="list"`, `display: contents` inside the grid, its items `role="listitem"`, the squeezed tracks' captions among them in reading order), each card a list of its containers named "Stack C, 4 containers, Loot Corner"; one card is the tab stop (roving), the arrow keys move to the nearest card that way on the plan's grid (the map's own `stackKeys`, which asks `planNeighbor` there and `nearestInDirection` on the map), Enter or Space selects, Esc clears. While searching, a matching card has a gold outline, its matching rows a gold bar on a light gold wash and a line naming the items found (cut with …, the whole line in its title), its other rows dimmed; cards with no match fade; the pills count the matches ("3 of 27"); the panel lists the matches as on the map.

**Show on map** (issue #10): an Inventory item's ⋯ menu (after "Show everything in this container") and a Containers view row's ⋯ menu offer it, with the house's name as the hint, when the item's container on the floor stands in a drawn house or on the plain grid. `ui/house-links.mts` knows which, and is the one such index (the search's elsewhere note reads it too): `GET /api/houses` lists each house's container serials (fetched with every inventory load and whenever the map fetches the houses, no house model needed), and any other ground container with a place is on the plain grid (`houseIndex`). It goes to `#/map/<house>?select=<serial>`: the map opens that house and level with the stack selected and zoomed to, and nothing else; the route then keeps only the house.

**The contents drawer** (issue #10): a chest row's **Show items** opens a fourth pane, `#map-drawer` (a labeled region, "Contents of <code> <name>"), instead of leaving for the Inventory. It never covers anything: it is a column of its own after the panel, 400 px by default and resizable (see **Its width** below), growing from no width (`.entering`, `--duration-slow`, none under reduced motion) while the map column narrows. Between 1100 and 1799 px the 280 px levels pane folds away while the drawer is open (its width, padding and border shrink to nothing with the same transition, then it is hidden), so the map keeps some room; the top bar's level pills still switch levels, and the pane comes back when the drawer closes. In the one column below 1100 px everything stacks, the drawer after the panel. Closed, the page is the three panes. It follows the selection: another stack (a click, or Enter on a focused one) swaps it to that stack's top opened chest (`drawerChest`), another chest's Show items to that chest, clearing the selection (Esc, Back to the house, a level, an area) closes it; the chest shown has an accent bar and `aria-current` in the panel. Header: the code and name (the label's, else the in-game name), "In game: <name> · Stack X, N containers" (`drawerMeta`), ✕ (Close contents), the fill meter ("n of N slots", `slotsText`) and the summary ("n items · a loose, b in k containers", `contentsSummary`); the chest picker (`drawerPicker`): up to four chests a `segmented` tab each (code over name; a chest not opened yet is disabled, titled "Not opened yet"), and for a taller stack a compact select labeled **Container** (`#map-drawer-chest`), an option per chest with its code and name, a chest not opened yet disabled and marked "Not opened yet" (the segmented control is for 2 to 4 choices); resist numbers on an item's second line are in the body face with tabular figures (mono is for identifiers); a filter on name, tooltip lines, rarity, kind and tags (`filterContents`, client-side, kept from chest to chest) and **Open in Inventory** (`showContainer`). Items come from `GET /api/items?root=<serial>` in 500-row pages, each serial kept once (the route's existing `root` filter, which matches every bag inside the chest), one fetch per chest shown and per rescan; `contentsOf` builds the tree once per fetch (the filter only filters it), items in a container cycle shown loose: bags first as `<details>` groups with their item count (nested inside nested, a closed bag stays closed through a redraw), then the items, each a button of two lines built from the Inventory's pieces (`rarityEl`, `tagEls`, peek.mts's `propertyLines` and `RESISTS`), its item tooltip on hover and focus (which shows whatever a cut line leaves out; bags carry theirs too); a click opens the Inventory row's ⋯ menu (`itemMenu`, whose Open details, anywhere but the Inventory's own Items view, opens the Inventory with the item in the peek). At a row's right end, shown on hover and focus and transparent (still in the tab order) otherwise, are the Inventory row's own actions (`inventory.mts`'s `itemActions`: Highlight in game, Grab to backpack, Go to container, gated by `bridgeActionReason` with the same disabled reasons, and More actions) in a `data-no-tip` zone; the drawer redraws on `bridgechange`. **Its width**: a handle on its left edge (`#map-drawer-grip`, `role="separator"`, vertical, "Resize contents", with `aria-valuenow`/`min`/`max`) drags it, ←/→ move the edge 16 px (64 with Shift; ← widens), Home and End go to the minimum and maximum, and a double-click chooses 400 px again (saved as 400, shown as wide as the window allows). It is at least 320 px and at most what leaves the map 360 px (`house-map-model.mts`'s `drawerMax`, `clampDrawer`, `drawerKey`), clamped before it grows open (measured with nothing animating, so a width saved on a wider screen never squeezes the map for a frame) and again whenever the page changes size (a ResizeObserver on `#map-body`); the save waits for a 300 ms pause; the chosen width is the ui-prefs `mapDrawerWidth` (a whole number from 320 to 4000, applied at load by `applyMapPrefs`) and reaches the layout as `--drawer-w` on `#map-body`. The map keeps its viewBox (the SVG scales it into the narrower pane, so the house never leaves the view) and its pills follow through their ResizeObserver. Below 1100 px the drawer stacks and has no handle. A new scan fetches the chest again and keeps the tab, the filter, the closed bags and the scroll. Esc anywhere inside closes it; closing puts focus back on the Show items that opened it, else on the selected stack; a redraw keeps focus on the same drawer control (every one has an id). Tab order is the panel, then the drawer.
